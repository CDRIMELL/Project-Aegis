### Wikipedia

`aircraft-attributes.wikipedia.json` holds individual numeric values extracted from the
specification templates of English Wikipedia articles. Each entry names the article, the revision
id and the revision timestamp it was taken from, which is the attribution. The file is made
available under the
[Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International licence](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/),
the licence of its source. Article text is not copied. Wikipedia is a tertiary source: values are
recorded as source-asserted, not as authoritative.

### Individually cited figures

`aircraft-characteristics.json` holds single published figures (a mass, a speed, a fuel capacity),
each recorded with the publisher's name, the URL and the text as published. Publishers include the
Royal Air Force and aircraft manufacturers. No text, image or table is reproduced beyond the short
quotation that identifies the figure. Content from UK government publishers is used under the
[Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/)
where the publisher states it applies. Entries are marked unverified until cross-checked.

Some entries in this file are single figures taken by hand from an English Wikipedia article where
the automatic extraction could not read them. Each names the article and revision, and is made
available under CC BY-SA 4.0 like the file above.

## Simulated content

Aircraft identifiers, the operator, missions, events and everything that happens in the simulated
world are fictional and generated by AEGIS. They are not reference data and describe no real
organisation, aircraft or operation.
